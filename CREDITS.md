## MRI imaging

**Adapted from:** Cancer Moonshot Biobank. (2022). *Cancer Moonshot Biobank – Multiple Myeloma Collection (CMB-MML)*
(Version 11) [dataset]. The Cancer Imaging Archive. <https://doi.org/10.7937/szkb-sw39> — the open radiology images of
the collection are released under **CC BY 4.0**.

> Data used in this publication were generated by the National Cancer Institute's Cancer Moonshot Biobank.

One de-identified research participant (collection subject `MSB-00498`), one thoracic spine MRI study on a 1.5 T
closed-bore system with the table's spine array. Series used:

- `SCOUT TSPINE` — 3-plane gradient-echo scout (360 mm FOV, 256 matrix, 5 mm) → the **localiser**
- `Sag T2 TSPINE`, `Sag T1 TSPINE`, `Sag STIR TSPINE` — 19 slices, 3 mm every 4 mm, 340 mm FOV → the **sagittal** images
- `Ax T2 STACK TSPINE`, `Ax T1 STACK TSPINE` — 26 slices, 4 mm every 5 mm, 200 mm FOV, tilted 7.1° → the **axial** images
- `Sag T1 Counting Loc` — 11 slices, 3 mm every 4 mm, 480 mm FOV → the **counting localiser**

The post-contrast series of the study (`POST SAG T1 FS`, `POST Ax T1 FS-DISC`) and the second scout are **not used**.

The clinical request in the simulator (mid-thoracic back pain) is **fictional**. Nothing in the simulator describes or
interprets what the images show.

### Before this goes public

- The collection has two tiers: open images (CC BY 4.0) and "images of the head", which are under the NIH Controlled
  Data Access Policy and may not be redistributed. The counting localiser includes the skull base and the back of the
  head. **Confirm that this study was downloaded from the open (CC BY 4.0) tier** before publishing these images.
- The vertebral levels were counted and marked without a radiologist. **Radiologist / radiographer review is
  outstanding.**

### Changes made

1. Pixel values were windowed to 8-bit and saved as JPEG (series) and PNG (localisers). The axial images were
   resampled from 512 to 384 pixels. Nothing was cropped, filtered or retouched.
2. **Localiser.** One image per plane of the study's own scout is used (the scout holds five per plane).
3. **The counting localiser was registered.** The study was acquired in two table positions, so the counting
   localiser's DICOM coordinates don't agree with the T-spine series. `tools/register.py` matches a mid-line slice of
   it to a mid-line slice of the sagittal T1 (rotation by brute force, shift by masked normalised cross-correlation,
   scored on the band holding the vertebral column) and the counting localiser's geometry is stored in the frame of
   the T-spine series: a rigid move of 6.25° in the sagittal plane plus a shift. The patient lay slightly differently
   the second time, so the two agree to a few millimetres along the spine (disc levels within ≈ 3 mm), not exactly.
4. **Vertebral levels.** Disc positions were found as intensity minima along the straightened vertebral column on
   the mid-line sagittal T1 images, then numbered by counting down from C2 on the counting localiser
   (`tools/landmarks.json`, ± 5 mm). The skin marker lies at the same height in both table positions, which supports
   the match. Level labels are shown as **?** in the simulator until the counting localiser has been scanned with C2
   in its FOV.
5. The geometry of every image (position, orientation, pixel size) is kept in `assets/calib.json` and drives the
   planning overlays. Six further landmarks (left and right foramina, front of the vertebral body, cord, spinous
   process, the dens) were picked by hand (± 5 mm).
6. In the browser: the localiser k-space animation is a real Fourier transform of the scout images; each scan shows
   the acquired slices that fall inside your stack, cropped to your FOV. Your angle doesn't re-slice the images — the
   slices are shown as acquired, and your axial tilt is graded against the tilt the block was acquired at
   (7.1°, ± 5°). The patient position chosen at set-up doesn't change the images.
7. The 3-D room shows a generic closed-bore magnet, not the real system.
8. Sequences other than those listed above are named for teaching only. There are no images for them.

Educational simulation. It isn't a medical device and isn't for clinical use.
